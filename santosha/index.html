Yama & Niyama

Niyama · The Second Observance · 07

सन्तोष

Santośa

Contentment

A state of proper ease — the mind at rest not because it has everything, but because it has stopped chasing.

Scroll

The word

Two kinds of ease, one syllable apart.

तोष

Tośa

Mental ease. The pleasure of getting the thing you wanted. It is real, and it lasts about an hour. Then the mind drops it and runs after the next.

सन्तोष

Santośa

Proper ease. The settled calm underneath, which no new acquisition can add to and no loss can take away. The prefix san​- means complete.

Contentment is not a feeling you reach by getting more. It is what is left when the chasing stops.

The law of nature

Human desire knows no end.

Through endless analysis the objects of enjoyment keep multiplying — in number and in abstraction — so the mental flow never gets any rest. Achieving a desired object pleases you for an hour; then the long-cherished thing loses its importance the moment it is held, and the mind moves on. This is not your weakness. It is the rule.

The millionaire

$1,000,000

…wants to become a multimillionaire

“Where is the money? I am somehow pulling on.”

— his answer, when asked if he is happy with his wealth. As money becomes the be-all and end-all, the mind is crudified; health is neglected; the body made unfit.

The practice

Be contented with the earnings of normal labour.

Without undue pressure on body or mind. Staying there takes a special effort — to keep aloof from external allurements, and to meet each pull of greed with the thought that opposes it.

The method

Two forces can pull a craving out of the mind.

Auto-suggestion

From within

You yourself, again and again, hold the thought that is the opposite of the mean tendency occupying the mind. Held long enough, your very nature changes.

Outer-suggestion

From outside

The same change, brought about by an ideal repeated into your ears by some external agent. Useful — but it depends on someone else doing the repeating.

For Santośa, only the first will do. No one can be content on your behalf.

What it is not

Contentment is not surrender.

It does not mean letting yourself be exploited and bearing it in silence. Never give up your right to self-preservation, or your legitimate dues — go on fighting, with concerted effort, to establish them. The line is not drawn between fighting and yielding. It is drawn between need and greed.

Where it leads

Still the chase, and the energy it wasted is freed.

Santośa is the inner face of Aparigraha — non-accumulation — and it clears the restless mind for Iishvara Prańidhána, shelter in the Cosmic. Contentment was never the absence of striving. It is striving aimed at the one thing that does not run out.
